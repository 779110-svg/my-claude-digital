02 — Project Brief

What this project asked for

For this project, I have been asked to create a digital portfolio about myself. The purpose of the portfolio is to introduce myself and show my personality, interests, skills, achievements and digital abilities.

It includes information about me, my interests, my skills, my education and any relevant achievements. I used digital design techniques to make it clear, organised and visually appealing.

It is aimed at my teacher and other people who may want to learn more about me, so it needs to be easy to navigate, use a consistent design and look professional.

03 — Research

Looking at other portfolios

Before building my own portfolio, I looked at a few other portfolio websites to get some ideas.

Adobe Portfolio

I liked the clean layout and simple navigation because it made the information easy to find, although I felt the colour scheme could have been more interesting.

Wix Portfolio Maker

I liked how the site used images and sections to present its work, but some pages felt a little crowded.

Google Sites ePortfolio

I liked the professional colour scheme and clear headings, which made the site easy to read and navigate.

What I took from this: keep navigation simple, use clear headings, make the colours more interesting than a template, and avoid crowding the pages.

04 — Planning (Learning Aim A)

Audience & purpose

The audience for my digital portfolio will mainly be potential employers, teachers and people who are interested in my skills and work. They could be around 16–60 years old and will have different levels of IT knowledge, from knowing a little to knowing a lot about IT.

I think most people will spend around five minutes looking through my portfolio, so I need to make sure it is easy to find things. They could view it on a computer, laptop, tablet or phone, but I would recommend a computer because it gives a better experience. They will want to see my skills, creativity, experiences, achievements and examples of my work, so it needs to look professional, be easy to navigate and work well on different devices.

Teachers / assessors

Clear structure and easy-to-find evidence; professional and well organised.

Employers / recruiters

Show my skills and progress quickly, in about five minutes.

Students

Simple design that is easy to understand.

05 — Design Decisions

Two design ideas, compared

I created two different design ideas for my portfolio. Design 1 uses navigation along the top of the page. Design 2 uses a sidebar navigation on the left.

That is why this website uses a sidebar: it stays on screen wherever you scroll, so a busy recruiter can jump to any section in one click.

Justification — every decision linked to the user

Layout

A clean, structured layout with one idea per section and plenty of spacing. User experience: people scan rather than read, so short sections stop them getting lost. Employer need: a recruiter with five minutes can find skills, education and goals without hunting.

Navigation

A consistent sidebar on every page with the same labels in the same order. User experience: users learn it once and never have to re-learn it; the highlighted link shows where they are. Employer need: reaching any part of the portfolio in one click saves time.

Typography

Two clear, simple fonts: Space Grotesk for headings and Inter for body text, at readable sizes. User experience: clear letterforms reduce eye strain and suit users with different levels of IT confidence. Employer need: looks professional while still being quick to read.

Colour

Black background with dark green cards, bright green highlights and yellow buttons; white text. User experience: strong contrast keeps text readable, and bright colours are used only for highlights and buttons so they guide the eye instead of overwhelming it. Employer need: a bold, memorable identity that is still consistent and professional.

Buttons

Clear, high-contrast yellow buttons with plain labels like “Start with About Me”. User experience: users instantly know what to click and what will happen. Employer need: makes it easy to take the next step.

Images & media

Relevant images and project examples, with alt text. User experience: adds visual interest and proof of my work. Employer need: shows skills with evidence rather than just claiming them.

Assets

Only free-to-use or original assets, each referenced with a source and licence (see Tools & Assets). Employer need: shows I understand copyright and work professionally.

Accessibility

  • Good colour contrast so text is easy to read
  • Readable font sizes and simple fonts
  • Clear, easy-to-understand buttons
  • Simple navigation so recruiters find information quickly
  • Works on different screen sizes — the sidebar becomes a top bar on phones
  • Clear headings and consistent navigation
  • Alt text on images
  • Animations switch off for users who prefer reduced motion

Final justification

I chose the second design because the navigation is clearer and makes it easier for users to find my different units and evidence. The simple layout keeps the focus on my work instead of making the pages too complicated. I used clear fonts and a consistent colour scheme so the portfolio is easy to read and looks professional.

My target audience includes teachers, assessors and potential employers, so I wanted the portfolio to show my work clearly and make it easy to find specific evidence. I also considered accessibility by using readable text, clear headings, good colour contrast and consistent navigation. Overall, my final design meets the purpose of my digital portfolio because it presents my work in an organised and professional way while making it easy for my audience to navigate and understand my skills and progress.

Colour scheme

My first colour idea was green. I then added black, yellow and white because I wanted a modern, bold and distinctive identity. Yellow draws attention to important information, headings and buttons. Green is a secondary colour that separates sections. Black is the main background because it creates strong contrast with the brighter colours. Bright colours are used selectively rather than covering large areas of text, so my teacher can read and navigate easily, and the same colours on every page keep the site connected.

06 — Project Plan

My project timeline

I split building my portfolio into four stages so it was easier to keep track of what I'd done.

1. Preparation

  • Identify the target audience
  • Decide the purpose
  • Research different portfolio designs
  • Plan the pages and navigation
  • Decide on colours, fonts and style
  • Collect images, icons and assets
  • Create initial storyboard designs

2. Development

  • Create the homepage and navigation
  • Create the About Me section
  • Add skills, education and personal information
  • Create sections for course units and projects
  • Add examples of my work
  • Create my Personal Achievements section
  • Add project descriptions and contact links

3. Testing & Feedback

  • Test all navigation buttons and links
  • Check information is easy to find
  • Check different screen sizes
  • Check text is readable
  • Ask my teacher and other users for feedback
  • Record problems or improvements

4. Refinement

  • Fix broken links or navigation problems
  • Improve layout and readability
  • Make changes based on feedback
  • Check colours and fonts are consistent
  • Remove anything unnecessary
  • Final check before submitting
